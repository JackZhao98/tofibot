# Web research

## What good looks like

- The answer gives the facts and the pages behind them. Each link is the specific page that states the fact: the article, the filing or the official page. A home, section, channel, tag or search page is never cited, and a story that was not opened is not reported.
- Timing is stated when it matters. When an event happened or a page was published is kept apart from when it was retrieved, and current data is kept apart from delayed data. Explicit timezones are converted and stated dates are kept.
- Figures the person asked for are computed from data already fetched (quotes, charts, tables).
- Confirmed facts and guesses are worded differently inside the sentences themselves. A separate caveat line is left out, and plain opinions carry none.
- Prior findings are leads. Fresh evidence comes from pages opened in this run.

## Effort

- A roundup needs one good listing page plus the pages of the items that are reported: about 3-5 page reads in total, with no cross-checking of every date.
- A scripted fetch is a quick first try. When the run has a Bot computer and a site refuses the fetch, the page is read in the real browser before it is called unreachable.
